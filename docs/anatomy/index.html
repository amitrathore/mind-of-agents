A conceptual map of shared agency

Anatomy of a
mind of agents.

A mind is more than a model and more than a collection of agents. It is a living arrangement for noticing, remembering, deciding, acting, and learning together.

Twelve design lenses for minds made of independent, interconnected agents.
The big picture

A body for
shared agency.

Picture a person: eyes notice, memory supplies history, a heart holds purpose, a spine holds the rules, hands act, and experience changes the next move. The analogy makes the architecture easier to explore. It does not suggest that an AI system has a human body or consciousness.

Every part below can be built in different ways. What matters is how the parts work together and how the mind relates to the independent people and agents within it.

THE FACE

Identity

A stable way to know which person, AI agent, group, or mind is involved—and on whose behalf it acts.

Design question

Can you trace an action to an actor and the authority behind it?

The connecting lines are as important as the numbered parts. A mind has relationships to its members, other minds, and the world. A shared mind can itself participate in another mind. No single component owns the whole.

The re/active loop

From signal to next move.

A mind gains continuity when each cycle can inform the next.

  1. 01

    Notice

    Perception and attention select an event worth handling.

  2. 02

    Understand

    Identity, context, meaning, and memory establish what it means here.

  3. 03

    Imagine

    Agents propose paths and compare possible outcomes.

  4. 04

    Decide

    Purpose, authority, and protocols determine what may happen.

  5. 05

    Act

    Capabilities change the world within time and resource limits.

  6. 06

    Learn

    Outcomes, provenance, and reflection update what the mind remembers.

Then the next signal arrives. The loop repeats, and relationships can change along with it.

A design example

What crosses
the boundary?

One way to make a decision legible across a group is to record a move: who acted, for what purpose, under whose authority, with what context and evidence, and what followed. This sketch is an example for designers to question and adapt, not a required format.

Links between such records could reveal paths of decisions and contributions. A group could inspect those paths while allowing participants to use different tools and systems.

Three design commitments

Keep the mind
open to its members.

01

People remain responsible.

Delegation should show who authorized an AI agent, what it may do, and how that authority can be revoked.

02

Members remain independent.

A person or agent can join several shared minds. Participation should not erase identity, ownership, or the ability to leave.

03

Evidence travels with outcomes.

Keep a useful account of who contributed, what was used, what happened, and what remains uncertain. Value sharing is a governance choice, not an automatic fact.

Ideas and influences

A model to build on.

This anatomy is a way to think about shared agency, informed by research on memory, reasoning, feedback, and provenance. The body metaphor organizes useful questions; builders can choose different parts, relationships, and interfaces.

Existing open standards can carry parts of the design: A2A addresses exchange between independent AI agents, while MCP exposes tools and context. Neither choice is required by this conceptual model.
